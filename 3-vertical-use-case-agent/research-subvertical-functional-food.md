## Headlines

1. **Protein moved from the gym into ordinary groceries, fast.** Swedish grocery sales of protein products tripled from 415 MSEK in 2022 to 1.2 BSEK in 2026 and are closing on energy drinks in value. [1][2]
2. **High-protein whey is the tightest ingredient in the category.** US prices are at record highs, the spot market for new buyers is close to gone, and meaningful new capacity lands in 2027 at the earliest. [10][12][13]
3. **Margins are being squeezed from both ends.** Input costs are up while promotions deepen: BellRing's gross margin fell from 35.4% to 28.6% in a year, and Celsius's fell 3.4 points on promotional spend. [11][54]
4. **Reformulation is now a structural cost of doing business, not a project.** Protein stresses taste, texture, shelf life and processing, and most developers are trying to add protein and cut cost at the same time. [19][20]
5. **The EU does not allow the loose wellness language the US tolerates.** Stockholm's food control ordered Nocco to change its website, and consumer campaigners file complaints brand by brand. In the US the risk is class actions instead. [33][34][36]
6. **Energy drink rules are splitting country by country across Northern Europe.** Norway: legal 16 age limit since 1 Jan 2026. England: ban for under-16s from April 2027. Sweden: voluntary 15 limit, new caffeine advice, retailers asking for a law. [39][40][41][43]
7. **Brands are buying their factories.** Vitamin Well (Barebells, Nocco) is merging with its bar manufacturer EMPWR because demand outran supply. [52]
8. **Big food buys functional growth rather than building it.** PepsiCo bought Poppi for $1.95bn, Celsius bought Alani Nu for $1.8bn, and PepsiCo's own portfolio is moving to protein and fibre. [35][53][55]
9. **Plant protein carries its own risks: contamination findings and trade duties.** Consumer Reports found plant protein powders averaged nine times the lead of dairy ones, and the EU put provisional duties of 40.5 to 67.4% on Chinese pea protein in April 2026. [30][17]
10. **Regulatory approval is slow and uneven.** EU novel food approval averages 937 days, so companies launch in the US first. The US, meanwhile, is moving to end self-affirmed GRAS. [47][45]
11. **Product data lives in too many systems.** Food companies use several applications for what PLM should cover, 40% name disconnected systems as their main barrier, and only 9% say they are fully connected. [61][62]
12. **Demand is strong but its durability is contested.** "Protein fatigue" is openly debated, Nordic consumers are more sceptical than US ones, and fibre is being positioned as "the next protein". [3][25][28]

## Part 1. Market shift

**R-01. Swedish grocery protein sales tripled in four years.** Sales of protein products in Swedish grocery went from 415 MSEK (2022) to 1.2 BSEK (2026). Protein drinks grew 42% over the period. YouGov's Charlotte Alring: "Consumers are increasingly choosing products they associate with strength and recovery. This will be noticed in sales and shelf space prioritization going forward." _SE. High. Survey + Trade press._ [1][2]

**R-02. Energy drinks show saturation in Sweden while protein grows.** The same YouGov data shows consumers do not plan to increase energy drink consumption over the next 12 months, while protein products are the category most consumers plan to buy more of. _SE. Medium. Survey via trade press._ [1][2]

**R-03. Protein has gone mainstream beyond fitness.** Skånemejerier's Proteinrapporten 2025: 270,000 tonnes of protein products consumed a year in Sweden, 4 in 10 Swedes buy protein-enriched products regularly, drinks and bars grow fastest, plant-based options rising among young people and women. "Protein har klivit ur gymväskan och in i vardagen." _SE. Medium. Survey commissioned by a producer._ [4]

**R-04. Kvarg is the Swedish protein dairy winner, and origin matters.** Kvarg grew 34% in H1 2026 and 75% over three years (Nielsen). Among kvarg buyers: 77% say Swedish origin is important, 47% choose on taste, 40% on satiety, 64% want low or no added sugar (68% of parents). _SE. Medium. Survey (Verian) commissioned by a producer._ [5]

**R-05. Protein bars: crowded and consolidating.** Swedish protein bar category grew 14% over 12 months to Sept 2025, with 19 companies competing for shelf space and larger groups buying smaller protein brands. _SE. Low (paywalled, partial read). Trade press._ [6]

**R-06. Big Nordic dairies are growing on protein.** Arla H1 2026: Arla Skyr +39.6% and Arla Protein +34.4% volume-driven revenue, Arla Foods Ingredients revenue +19.3% to EUR 867m, branded growth guidance raised to 4 to 6%. Valio's PROfeel range reached EUR 100m net sales in 2025, sold in 15 countries including Sweden, with a US pudding launch that beat expectations. _Nordics. High. Primary._ [8][9]

**R-07. Launches now target everyday occasions, not athletes.** Arla launched ShakeUP (12 g protein, lactose-free, added fibre and vitamins) in May 2026 for young people who skip meals, rolling out at Axfood, Coop and Reitan in week 19 and ICA in week 37. Arla cites that only 7% of young people eat according to Livsmedelsverket's guidelines. _SE. High. Primary._ [7]

**R-08. The US consumer push is bigger and broader.** 70% of Americans want more protein, up from 59% four years earlier (IFIC). Circana: 23% of US households have a GLP-1 user, and those households are projected to account for 35% of food and beverage units by 2030; they seek more protein, fibre and healthy fats and buy less sugar and carbohydrate. CoBank's lead dairy economist Corey Geiger reports protein shake sales up 71% over four years and a 12% price premium on high-protein labelled products. _US. High. Survey + Trade press._ [10][15][26]

**R-08b. GLP-1 use is expected to last, which changes what "for GLP-1 users" means.** FoodNavigator on oral GLP-1s: "GLP-1s are evolving into a long-term behaviour modifier. That has profound implications for food and beverage companies" (Dr Simon Cork). Suggested product responses: protein and fibre density, micronutrients such as calcium and vitamin D, digestibility, smaller eating occasions. _EU, Global. Medium. Trade press._ [29]

**R-09. GLP-1 use is high in Sweden and Denmark, but so is scepticism of protein snacks.** YouGov: 7.5% of Swedish and 9.1% of Danish households use a GLP-1 medication, 13 to 15% are open to it. Only 4 to 6% of Swedes and Danes see high-protein snacks or shakes as a good investment; 40 to 47% call them a waste of money. _SE, DK. Medium. Survey._ [28]

**R-10. Innova ranks protein and gut health as the top two global trends for 2026.** "Powerhouse Protein" is #1, "Gut Health Hub" #2, "Beverages with Purpose" #4, "Mind Balance" #8. Nearly 60% of global consumers are actively adding protein; 40% cite "natural or minimally processed" as a key protein consideration. _Global. Medium. Survey firm press release._ [24]

**R-11. Functional coffee is a live format.** At Vitafoods Europe 2026, suppliers cited Starbucks protein foam and protein coffee as a mainstream signal; Carbery: "Protein coffee is hot at the moment." Valio: "Consumers are paying more attention to the quality of protein over the quantity." _EU, US. Medium. Trade press._ [22]

**R-12. Official dietary advice pulls in two directions.** The US Dietary Guidelines 2025 to 2030 recommend 1.2 to 1.6 g protein per kg body weight a day, well above the old 0.8 g RDA, and nutrition scientists have questioned whether the evidence supports that for everyone. Livsmedelsverket's 2025 advice instead tells Swedes to eat peas, beans and lentils often, preferably every day, and cap red and processed meat at 350 g cooked a week. _US, SE. High. Primary + professional body._ [50][51]

**R-13. Fibre is being positioned as the next protein.** PepsiCo CEO Ramon Laguarta said fibre could be "the next protein". PepsiCo's Q2 2026 results credit growth to "functional benefits such as hydration, protein and fiber". _US, Global. High. Primary + Trade press._ [25][55]

## Part 2. Sourcing

**R-14. Whey prices at records, suppliers sold out.** Standard whey powder prices up more than 50% since January 2026 (DCA Market Intelligence). WPC80 spot above $11 per lb, WPI "firmly in the $12s" (USDA). Some suppliers sold out for the rest of 2026 (USDA). BellRing's CEO called whey prices "historic highs". _US. High. Trade press citing USDA._ [10]

**R-15. The market is built on forward contracts, so newcomers are locked out.** University of Wisconsin extension: high-protein whey "grew up largely as a relationship business built on negotiated, forward contracts", producers have "sold forward well into 2026", and US WPC and WPI are "essentially unavailable to new buyers seeking significant volume." Two-year cost increases: WPC about +108%, WPI about +139%. The bottleneck is "the ultrafiltration and spray-drying equipment that turns ordinary whey into high-protein whey"; only about 1% of liquid whey becomes high-protein grades. _US, Global. High. Primary (university)._ [12]

**R-16. Capacity is coming, mostly in 2027.** Glanbia: about 4,500 t WPI, New Mexico, 2027. Tirlán: EUR 126m, Ireland, mid-2027. Arla Foods Ingredients: US contract manufacturing at Valley Queen (winter 2025/26) and WPC at Videbæk, Denmark (January 2027). DMK: 7,000 t WPC80, Germany, EUR 26m (May 2026). Saputo: +35% WPC80, CA$180m+. FrieslandCampina: doubled WPI at Borculo and $90m+ across three Dutch sites. Buyers are booking months ahead and some are using caseinates as a whey alternative. _EU, US, Nordics. High. Trade press._ [13][14]

**R-17. Arla and DMK merged into Europe's largest farmer-owned dairy.** Effective 1 June 2026: about 11,200 farmer owners and about 20 bn kg milk. Arla is also investing EUR 300m in a cheese dairy in Götene, Sweden, doubling intake to about 1 bn kg a year from 2030. More cheese means more whey. _SE, EU. High. Primary._ [8]

**R-18. Milk is cheap while protein is dear.** Arla's performance price fell to 43.6 EUR-cent/kg from 57.5, commodity butter, SMP and Gouda prices fell 27.3% on average, yet protein ingredients are at records. The value in milk has moved to the protein fraction. _EU. High. Primary + university._ [8][12]

**R-19. Plant protein: duties on the cheapest source.** The EU imposed provisional anti-dumping duties of 40.5 to 67.4% on Chinese pea protein above 65% protein (Implementing Regulation (EU) 2026/916, 27 April 2026). The US was already acting: in 2022, five US pea protein buyers sourced 96.6% from China. _EU, US. High. Primary + Trade press._ [17][18]

**R-20. Nordic plant protein is being built locally.** Lantmännen is investing SEK 1.2bn in a pea and faba protein plant in Lidköping: 7,000 t protein isolate a year from 35,000 t peas and 5,000 t faba beans, due H1 2027, with a goal of lifting Swedish pea cultivation 50% from 80,000 to 120,000 t. _SE. High. Trade press reporting a primary release._ [16]

**R-21. Nordic ingredient firms sell application know-how, not just protein.** Valio offers high-protein, low-sugar application concepts; Apetit's BlackGrain texturised protein from rapeseed press cake took four years to novel food authorisation (developed from 2017, authorised 2021). _FI, Nordics. Medium. Trade press._ [21]

**R-22. Plant protein carries a contamination sourcing risk.** Consumer Reports found plant-based protein products averaged nine times more lead than dairy ones; Vega moved its pea protein sourcing from China to North America after the findings. _US. Medium. Advocacy (consumer testing)._ [30]

**R-23. Cocoa hits coated bars.** Cocoa peaked near $13,000/t in late 2024 and was still $5,000 to $6,000/t in early 2026. Manufacturers are shifting to compound coatings, alternative fats and cocoa-free analogues, "as core strategy rather than contingency". _Global. Medium. Trade press._ [49]

**R-24. Deforestation due diligence arrives for cocoa and coffee.** EUDR applies from 30 December 2026 for large and medium operators and 30 June 2027 for micro and small ones, covering cocoa, coffee, soy, palm oil, cattle, rubber and wood. Simplified duties for micro, small and downstream businesses were adopted in December 2025. _EU. High. NGO explainer tracking the law._ [48]

**R-25. Energy, freight and can costs add pressure.** Arla cites cost inflation from the Middle East crisis; Valio's CEO expects energy, freight and related costs to rise if the conflict continues; Celsius reports aluminium costs rising. _Nordics, US. High. Primary._ [8][9][54]

## Part 3. Operations and manufacturing

**R-26. Protein reformulation is a structural challenge.** Kerry's Elizabeth Horvath: "Protein is hard. It stresses taste, texture, shelf life and processing, often forcing trade-offs." Protein dampens crunch, tightens crumb, affects browning and viscosity. About 70% of developers prioritise cost reduction while adding protein, about 66% prioritise clean label, and more than 60% of development activity involves reformulation. _Global. High. Trade press._ [19]

**R-27. Reformulation now means full redesign.** Suppliers report that MAHA-driven change in the US "now requires full product redesign, balancing taste, texture, safety, and more", while whey costs push formulators toward blends and alternatives. _US, Global. Medium. Trade press._ [23]

**R-28. Heat processing destroys trending functional ingredients.** UltraPhil's John Eaton: "A lot of functional ingredients that are trending have challenges in that the heat treatment of aseptic processes can severely degrade them." Commercial-scale plant trials cost upwards of $450,000, and pilot-scale aseptic testing lags innovation speed. _Global. Medium. Vendor quoted in trade press._ [20]

**R-29. Brands outgrow contract manufacturers.** Vitamin Well Group (Barebells, Nocco, Vitamin Well; about EUR 650m revenue, about 750 staff) is merging with EMPWR, its bar manufacturer (four plants in Croatia, the Netherlands, the US and Canada; 1,500+ staff; 100+ customers). Cinven: the deal "unlocks the ability to increase investments in capacity and pursue significant volume growth". Seventy8 Consulting's Karl Bickley: "When demand outruns supply, owning the brand stops being enough." _SE, EU, US. High. Trade press._ [52]

**R-30. Packing errors on fast-moving SKUs become allergen recalls.** Barebells SOFT Coco Choco bars were recalled in Finland (28 May 2026) because Peanut Cloud bars were packed in the wrapper, with undeclared peanut and soy, after a batch "sold in large quantities since early April" at sports venues, Wolt and K-Group stores. _FI. High. Primary (regulator)._ [31]

**R-31. Supplier quality failures hit the P&L.** BellRing took an $11.3m inventory write-off for a third-party supplied ingredient that failed quality standards. _US. High. Primary._ [11]

**R-32. Testing is inconsistent across the industry.** Of 23 protein products tested by Consumer Reports, more than two-thirds exceeded CR's lead level of concern per serving; only nine companies said they test both ingredients and finished products, and only two publish heavy metal results. The trade body CRN objected that CR's threshold is not a government limit. _US. Medium. Advocacy, with industry rebuttal._ [30]

**R-33. Sweden's food industry wants to grow but cannot hire.** Livsmedelsföretagen's Kompetensrapport 2026: 85% of members want to increase production over five years; 7 in 10 find recruiting harder than five years ago; 3 in 4 have suffered from failed recruitment. Hardest roles: automation technicians, industrial electricians, maintenance, process operators. Karin Thapper: "Kompetent personal är helt avgörande för industrins konkurrens- och motståndskraft." _SE. High. Trade body survey._ [58]

**R-34. Ingredient suppliers are investing in AI to speed development.** Cargill's Abhishek Roy: "We are embedding AI across the full innovation journey, from early consumer insight and concept development through formulation, scale-up, and execution" and "These tools require context, validation, and oversight." Goals: fewer physical trials, fewer failed launches, faster reformulation. _Global. Medium. Trade press._ [65]

## Part 4. Regulation and claims

**R-35. EU claims are a closed list.** Under Regulation 1924/2006 only authorised nutrition and health claims may be used. "Source of protein" requires at least 12% of energy from protein; "high protein" at least 20%. _EU. High. Primary (law)._ [32]

**R-36. Stockholm's food control has ruled against functional drink wording.** Stockholms stad found Nocco's website claims (suited to drinking during exercise, "functional drinks", justified by vitamins, amino acids and caffeine) not backed by authorised claims, and the company changed its website. "Varje påstående om ett livsmedels hälsoeffekt måste vara grundat i ett specifikt hälsopåstående som i sin tur har godkänts av unionen." Further complaints followed on the revised wording. _SE. High (decision reported by the complainant organisation). Advocacy._ [34]

**R-37. Campaigners file complaints brand by brand.** Food journalist Ann Fernholm filed complaints with Stockholm's food control against Vitamin Well, Barebells, Tyngre, Nobe and Nocco (all one group), including "Approved by Tyngre" marks on sister brands, and noted Barebells removed "functional foods" wording after an earlier complaint. _SE. Medium. Advocacy._ [33]

**R-38. In the US the claims risk is litigation.** Poppi's former owner settled a class action for $8.9m (no admission) alleging a consumer "would need to drink more than four cans of Poppi in a day to realize any gut health benefits". _US. High. Legal news._ [36]

**R-39. "GLP-1 friendly" has no regulatory definition.** Conagra put a "GLP-1 friendly" tag on Healthy Choice meals, Nestlé launched Vital Pursuit, Danone launched a yogurt drink for GLP-1 users. A packaging executive writing in Food Dive: "Did the product actually change, or is 'GLP-1 Friendly' simply a marketing lure?" _US. Medium. Trade press + opinion._ [26][27]

**R-40. Norway made a 16 age limit law from 1 January 2026.** An energy drink is any non-alcoholic drink with more than 15 mg caffeine per 100 ml ready to drink, powders and tablets included. Products based on coffee, tea or cocoa with those words in the product name are exempt. All sellers must check age, run internal control and register with Mattilsynet. The drinks industry association said an age limit is "neither necessary nor proportionate". _NO. High. Primary (regulator) + national broadcaster._ [41][42]

**R-41. England bans high-caffeine drinks for under-16s from April 2027.** Scope: drinks other than tea or coffee above 150 mg caffeine per litre, in all retail including online and vending, fines up to £2,500, enforced by local authorities. _UK. High. Primary._ [43]

**R-42. Sweden: advice, not law, and retail wants a law.** Livsmedelsverket's new advice (May 2026): under 16s at most 70 mg caffeine a day, under 6s none. Retailers (Axfood, Bergendahls, Coop, ICA, Lidl) have applied a voluntary 15 age limit since 2009 and kept it when Livsmedelsverket declined to propose a legal limit in 2018 [37]; in Feb 2026 Svensk Dagligvaruhandel called for a mandatory limit for all sellers after a 14-year-old bought energy drinks in 3 of 4 stores tested. Sweden has no caffeine maximum; nearly half of 25 energy drinks tested would be illegal under Danish and Norwegian caffeine rules. A standard Nocco can carries 180 mg caffeine, over two and a half times the new daily advice for under-16s. _SE. High. Regulator via trade press + public broadcaster._ [38][39][40]

**R-43. UK sugar levy extends to milk drinks in 2028.** From 1 January 2028 the Soft Drinks Industry Levy threshold drops from 5 g to 4.5 g sugar per 100 ml and covers pre-packaged milk-based and milk-alternative drinks with added sugar, including milkshakes, flavoured milks, sweetened yogurt drinks and ready-to-drink coffees. _UK. High. Primary._ [44]

**R-44. The US wants to end self-affirmed GRAS.** FDA's proposed rule (August 2026) would require notification for GRAS substances, with an 18-month compliance period and a 12-month streamlined window for substances already on the market; comments close 9 December 2026. Lawyers advise companies to inventory ingredients, check safety data, engage suppliers and consider reformulation. _US. High. Law firm summary of primary rule._ [45]

**R-45. EU novel food approval is slow enough to change launch order.** Average 937 days to authorisation, about 2.7 extra data requests per application, applicants spend about 47% of the time answering them; 86.8% end positive. One case: five years pending in the EU against under six months in the US on the same data. Companies respond by launching in the US first. _EU. Medium. Trade press citing a peer-reviewed analysis._ [47]

**R-46. US "healthy" claim rules change by 2028.** FDA's updated "healthy" rule (Dec 2024) requires food group equivalents and limits on added sugars, sodium and saturated fat; compliance date 25 February 2028. _US. High. Law firm summary of primary rule._ [46]

**R-47. US traceability deadline moved to 2028.** The FSMA 204 Food Traceability Rule compliance date is now 20 July 2028, after Congress directed FDA not to enforce earlier; lot-level tracking was the main feasibility concern. _US. High. Primary._ [68]

**R-55. ## Part 7. Challenges and how they are solved today

_Ranked by weight of evidence in this file. Each challenge lists the findings behind it._

### CH-01. Securing protein ingredients at a price the product can carry

- **What it is.** High-protein whey is sold forward to established buyers; new or growing brands face a thin, record-priced spot market until new capacity lands in 2027. Plant alternatives carry duties and contamination risk.
- **Who feels it.** Procurement and sourcing, R&D (who must reformulate), finance (margin), commercial (price increases).
- **How it shows up.** Gross margin collapse (BellRing 35.4% to 28.6%), price increases on powders (Dymatize +20.7% price/mix), suppliers "booked up months in advance", launches delayed or reformulated.
- **How it is solved today.** Forward contracts with existing suppliers; price increases; reformulating to blends, caseinates, milk protein concentrate, pea or egg; switching origin (Vega to North America); suppliers investing in capacity (Arla, DMK, Glanbia, Tirlán, Lantmännen); brands buying manufacturing (Vitamin Well and EMPWR).
- **Geo.** Strongest in the US; EU and Nordic supply is building capacity.
- **Evidence.** R-14, R-15, R-16, R-18, R-19, R-20, R-22, R-29, R-50. **Confidence: High.**

### CH-02. Adding protein and function without breaking the product

- **What it is.** Protein and functional ingredients damage taste, texture, shelf life and processability, while the same product must also cut sugar and cost and keep a clean label. Heat processing degrades trending functional ingredients.
- **Who feels it.** R&D and product development, quality, operations, co-manufacturers.
- **How it shows up.** More than 60% of development work is reformulation; "full product redesign"; commercial-scale trials above $450,000; reformulation pushed by input costs (cocoa, whey) as much as by consumers.
- **How it is solved today.** Flavour and seasoning masking (PepsiCo); ingredient suppliers selling ready application concepts (Valio, Kerry, Apetit); pilot-scale aseptic testing before commercial runs; compound coatings and alternative fats; early AI formulation tools at large suppliers (Cargill) with human validation.
- **Geo.** Global.
- **Evidence.** R-21, R-23, R-26, R-27, R-28, R-34. **Confidence: High.**

### CH-03. Saying what the product does without breaking claims law

- **What it is.** The EU allows only authorised claims with fixed thresholds; wellness wording common in the US ("functional", "recover", "focus", "gut healthy", "GLP-1 friendly") is either not allowed in the EU or exposes US brands to class actions.
- **Who feels it.** Marketing, regulatory affairs, legal, brand managers, e-commerce teams (websites count as commercial communication).
- **How it shows up.** Local food control decisions ordering website changes (Nocco); complaints filed brand by brand by campaigners; on-pack wording rewrites ("functional foods" removed at Barebells); US settlements (Poppi $8.9m); terms with no legal definition used on pack.
- **How it is solved today.** Mostly reactively: changing wording after a complaint or decision; legal review of claims; relying on authorised protein nutrition claims (12% / 20% energy); vendors now pitching real-time claim validation inside formulation software.
- **Geo.** SE and EU (enforcement by complaint), US (litigation).
- **Evidence.** R-35, R-36, R-37, R-38, R-39, R-58. **Confidence: High.**

### CH-04. Energy drink rules that differ by country

- **What it is.** The same can faces a legal 16 age limit in Norway (above 15 mg/100 ml), an under-16 ban in England from April 2027 (above 150 mg/l), caffeine caps in Denmark and Norway, and in Sweden only advice plus a voluntary retailer limit that retailers themselves want made law.
- **Who feels it.** Commercial and sales (channel access, age checks), regulatory, product development (caffeine levels per market), retail partners.
- **How it shows up.** Nearly half of energy drinks tested in Sweden would be illegal in Denmark and Norway; high-caffeine flagship SKUs (180 mg per can) set against 70 mg daily advice for under-16s; energy drink demand in Sweden flattening.
- **How it is solved today.** Retailer voluntary age checks; market-specific formulations; naming rules (Norway exempts products named as coffee, tea or cocoa drinks); portfolio shift toward protein, hydration and lower-caffeine lines.
- **Geo.** NO, SE, DK, UK.
- **Evidence.** R-02, R-40, R-41, R-42. **Confidence: High.**

### CH-05. Proving what is in the product

- **What it is.** Contaminants in plant proteins, ingredients failing supplier quality, and packing errors on fast-rotating SKUs, with inconsistent testing and disclosure across brands.
- **Who feels it.** Quality and food safety, procurement (supplier approval), operations (packing lines), brand and communications (recalls).
- **How it shows up.** Inventory write-offs ($11.3m at BellRing); allergen recalls from wrong-wrapper packing (Barebells, Finland); public test results naming brands (Consumer Reports).
- **How it is solved today.** Supplier certificates of analysis plus some third-party testing of ingredients and finished goods (9 of 23 brands tested said they do both); a few brands publish results; moving sourcing origin; recall and write-off when it fails.
- **Geo.** US strongest; recall example from FI.
- **Evidence.** R-22, R-30, R-31, R-32. **Confidence: Medium to High.**

### CH-06. Keeping up with ingredient approval and label rule changes

- **What it is.** Slow EU novel food approval, the proposed end of US self-affirmed GRAS, new US "healthy" criteria, the UK sugar levy on milk drinks, and a US traceability rule, each with its own deadline.
- **Who feels it.** Regulatory affairs, R&D, supply chain (supplier dossiers), finance (reformulation cost).
- **How it shows up.** US-first launches; ingredient inventories and supplier engagement exercises; reformulation of protein milkshakes and ready-to-drink coffee for the UK by 2028.
- **How it is solved today.** Launch sequencing by market; inventories of ingredient regulatory status; supplier dossiers; reformulation; legal counsel tracking deadlines.
- **Geo.** EU, UK, US.
- **Evidence.** R-43, R-44, R-45, R-46, R-47. **Confidence: Medium to High.**

### CH-07. Scaling production fast enough

- **What it is.** Demand grows faster than contract manufacturing, specialised processing (aseptic, filtration) and skilled labour.
- **Who feels it.** Operations, supply chain, co-manufacturers, HR, owners and investors.
- **How it shows up.** Brand owners buying their manufacturer; capacity booked ahead; Swedish producers unable to hire automation and maintenance technicians while planning to grow output.
- **How it is solved today.** Vertical integration (Vitamin Well and EMPWR); supplier capacity investments; pilot plants; automation, limited by the skills shortage.
- **Geo.** SE, EU, US.
- **Evidence.** R-16, R-20, R-28, R-29, R-33. **Confidence: Medium to High.**

### CH-08. Protecting margin while retailers and promotions squeeze

- **What it is.** Input inflation lands at the same time as heavier promotion, stronger retailer power, private label, and in Sweden a VAT cut that has to be fully passed through.
- **Who feels it.** Commercial and key account teams, revenue management, finance.
- **How it shows up.** Celsius margin down on promotions; Premier Protein price/mix negative; executives expecting power to shift to retailers (79%) and more private label (65%).
- **How it is solved today.** Promotions and trade spend; distribution partnerships (PepsiCo carrying Celsius brands); affordability initiatives; SKU rationalisation.
- **Geo.** US, SE, Global.
- **Evidence.** R-49, R-50, R-51, R-52, R-54. **Confidence: Medium to High.**

### CH-09. Betting on which functional trend lasts

- **What it is.** Protein demand is strong, but "protein fatigue" is debated, Nordic consumers are sceptical of processed protein snacks, official Swedish advice points to legumes over fortified products, GLP-1 users' needs shift, and fibre is rising.
- **Who feels it.** Strategy, portfolio and innovation leads, marketing, investors.
- **How it shows up.** Trade press asking "how long will it last"; big players adding fibre lines alongside protein; plant-based shakeout showing how fast a trend can reverse.
- **How it is solved today.** Hedging the portfolio (protein plus fibre plus hydration); portion-controlled formats; buying proven brands rather than building; tracking panel data (YouGov, Nielsen, Circana).
- **Geo.** SE, Nordics, US.
- **Evidence.** R-03, R-09, R-12, R-13, R-48, R-53, plus [3][25]. **Confidence: Medium.**

### CH-10. Product data spread across too many systems

- **What it is.** Formula, specification, supplier, label and regulatory data live in several applications and in spreadsheets and PDFs; retailers require validated product data; AI tools need connected data to work.
- **Who feels it.** R&D, regulatory, quality, master data teams, IT, and everyone preparing retailer product information.
- **How it shows up.** 40% name disconnected systems as their main barrier; 9% fully connected; informal use of public AI tools; retailer data checked attribute by attribute against the pack.
- **How it is solved today.** A mix of ERP, PLM and point solutions (Paulig: industry ERP plus PLM); GS1 Validoo quality assurance for retail data; spreadsheets; vendors adding AI to PLM and formulation.
- **Geo.** Global, SE.
- **Evidence.** R-55, R-56, R-57, R-58, R-59, R-60. **Confidence: Medium** (two of the four sources are vendors).

### CH-11. Trade barriers and volatile non-protein inputs

- **What it is.** Duties on key inputs (Chinese pea protein), US metal tariffs raising can costs, and volatile cocoa, energy and freight.
- **Who feels it.** Procurement, finance, supply chain.
- **How it shows up.** Supply chain shortening (69% of executives), price increases, reformulation away from expensive inputs, cost lines called out in results.
- **How it is solved today.** Nearshoring and local capacity (Lantmännen Swedish peas, Roquette in Canada); alternative fats and compound coatings; pricing.
- **Geo.** EU, US.
- **Evidence.** R-19, R-20, R-23, R-24, R-25, R-52. **Confidence: Medium.**
